## 2. What YouTube's monetization policy says: inauthentic content, reused content, mass-produced AI video, AI voice-over

### Takeaway
The rule is officially framed as old and unchanged in substance: content that is mass-produced, templated or repetitive is not eligible for monetization, and the penalty is channel-wide. It was renamed "inauthentic content" on 15 July 2025 and, per multiple secondary reports, reworded again on about 16 July 2026 into named categories (generic or repetitive; unsatisfying or off-putting; AI personas on sensitive topics). AI use and AI voices are not banned per se; templated sameness is the trigger.

### Cited Findings
**July 2025 update (official framing, relayed)**
- Reported official changelog wording: "We're making a minor update to our 'repetitious content' policy to better clarify that this includes content that is repetitive or mass-produced," and the policy is renamed "inauthentic content". Effective **15 July 2025** (announced early July). — [air.io dated timeline](https://air.io/en/monetization/youtube-monetization-policy-changes-2026-a-complete-dated-timeline); [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-monetization-update-spam-not-reaction-channels/550755/)
- YouTube's Rene Ritchie called it a "minor update"; TeamYouTube: "a minor update to our longstanding guideline", no new policies added; such content has long been ineligible and is often viewed as spam. — [getcoai summary](https://about.getcoai.com/news/youtube-cracks-down-on-ai-generated-spam-with-new-monetization-rules); [ppc.land](https://ppc.land/youtube-improves-detection-systems-for-unoriginal-content/)
- Target: mass-produced content, i.e. template-based videos with minimal variation; reported policy phrase: videos that look like they were "made with a template with little to no variation". — [ppc.land](https://ppc.land/youtube-improves-detection-systems-for-unoriginal-content/); [MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2025/07/223-youtube-monetisation-policy-repetitive-content/)
- Image slideshows or scrolling text with minimal or no narrative, commentary, or educational value are named as ineligible. — [MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2025/07/223-youtube-monetisation-policy-repetitive-content/)
- Enforcement is **channel-level**: monetization may be removed from the entire channel. — [ppc.land](https://ppc.land/youtube-clarifies-inauthentic-content-policy-changes/)
- AI-generated content is not banned; AI-assisted content can qualify with meaningful original input. — [ppc.land](https://ppc.land/youtube-clarifies-inauthentic-content-policy-changes/)
- The separate **reused content** policy was unchanged at the July 2025 rename; "reused" concerns other people's material without significant original commentary/modification, while "inauthentic" concerns your own videos looking mass-produced or repetitive. — [keepmonetized](https://keepmonetized.com/guides/reused-vs-inauthentic-content/)

**July 2026 update (secondary reports; details inconsistent)**
- TechCrunch headline, 20 July 2026: "YouTube clarifies policies around AI slop and upsetting videos"; changes affect all YouTube Partner Program members. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/20/youtube-clarifies-policies-around-ai-slop-and-upsetting-videos/)
- Reported rollout date 16 July 2026; "Inauthentic Content" reportedly renamed **"Generic or Repetitive Content"**; the policy page reportedly now has four sections: Generic or Repetitive Content, Reused Content, Unsatisfying or Off-putting Content, AI Personas Related to Sensitive Topics. **[UNVERIFIED — sources disagree on whether it is a rename or a three-way split]** — [peggyktc](https://www.peggyktc.com/2026/07/youtube-clarifies-inauthentic-content.html); [outlierkit July 2026 updates](https://outlierkit.com/blog/youtube-updates-july-2026); [scalelab](https://scalelab.com/en/youtube-monetization-policy-updates)
- Generic or repetitive: content that looks template-made, or that "may feel repetitive to viewers after watching several videos in a row from the same channel"; reported wording also mentions AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates giving the impression of mass production. — [newmoneymatrix](https://newmoneymatrix.org/youtube-inauthentic-content-policy-explained/); [faceless.my](https://faceless.my/youtube/youtube-inauthentic-content-policy-faceless-ai-2026/)
- Unsatisfying or off-putting: videos built to shock or manipulate; example given: repetitive scenarios of animals in peril, especially relying on generic templates. — [viddar](https://viddar.io/en/blog/youtube-inauthentic-content-policy-monetization)
- AI personas related to sensitive topics: AI personas presenting themselves as experts on **health, legal issues, finances or politics**. — [noobclaw](https://noobclaw.com/blog/youtube-inauthentic-content-policy-2026/)
- YouTube reportedly described the July 2026 change as communications-only: "The rules themselves haven't changed." — [channelterminated.com](https://channelterminated.com/demonetized/inauthentic-content/)
- Search Engine Journal ran "YouTube explains what can stop a channel getting paid" (2026; not read). — [SEJ](https://searchenginejournal.com/youtube-explains-what-can-stop-a-channel-getting-paid/583096)

**AI voice-over**
- Secondary sources agree the policy does not disqualify AI use (including TTS narration) by itself; creator complaints of demonetization cluster around "AI narration + third-party source material" and "AI visuals + static or weak editing". — [ytgrowth](https://ytgrowth.io/blog/youtube-ai-policy); [aituber.app](https://aituber.app/blog/faceless-youtube-channels-demonetized-2026/)

### Inferences
- A stick-figure channel is structurally exposed to the "generic or repetitive" test because the visual template is identical across videos by design; what must carry the variation is script, structure, topic depth, and narration. The "several videos in a row from the same channel" phrasing (if accurate) means reviewers judge the channel, not single videos.
- The "AI personas on sensitive topics" category is directly relevant to stick-figure **health and finance** explainers narrated by an AI voice posing as an expert (e.g. a "doctor stickman"); psychology framed as clinical advice may fall near it.
- Critics' reading that the rename widened enforcement from "same thing too many times" to "not genuinely human-created" is commentary, not official policy. — [thenextweb](https://thenextweb.com/news/youtube-ai-slop-crackdown-faceless-creators-collateral-damage)

### Gaps
- Verbatim official text of the current Help Center page could not be retrieved (host blocked). Exact July 2026 naming and wording must be re-checked at support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392.
- Background knowledge, not verified this session: the July 2025 Help Center note read approximately "On July 15, 2025, YouTube is updating our guidelines to better identify mass-produced and repetitious content. This update better reflects what 'inauthentic' content looks like today."
- No official statement specifically about AI voice-over and monetization was found; claims that "95% of ElevenLabs-voiced channels" are hit etc. come from creator videos and were discarded.

## 3. Demonetizations and terminations in 2025–2026; what distinguished survivors

### Takeaway
No report was found of stick-figure channels being hit as a category, but 2026 saw large terminations of AI "slop" channels and documented cases of genuinely hand-made animation channels being demonetized as "inauthentic content" with templated appeal responses. Animation with a repeating look is demonstrably at risk of false positives.

### Cited Findings
- Dec 2025 (approx.): YouTube terminated **Screen Culture and KH Studio** (2M+ subscribers, 1B+ combined views) for AI fake movie trailers. — [NetInfluencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/youtube-terminates-ai-generated-fake-movie-trailer-channels/)
- **January 2026**: 16 channels with a combined ~35 million subscribers and ~4.7 billion lifetime views were removed; a YouTube spokesperson attributed removals to **spam and deceptive practices policies** (i.e. existing rules, not a new anti-AI rule). Figure is consistent across outlets, but no primary YouTube statement with the numbers was found. — [IBTimes UK](https://www.ibtimes.co.uk/youtube-cracks-down-ai-generated-content-1800213); [Express Tribune](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2589980/youtube-cracks-down-deletes-47b-views-of-ai-spam-videos); [inkl](https://www.inkl.com/news/is-youtube-finally-cracking-down-on-ai-16-channels-with-35-million-subs-just-got-wiped-out)
- A Google system (reported as "S-CTS") terminated **50K clusters comprising 130K channels over six months**, targeting coordinated AI spam networks via content patterns and infrastructure signals; date range not confirmed. **[UNVERIFIED single outlet]** — [Outlook Respawn](https://respawn.outlookindia.com/gaming/gaming-news/google-ai-spam-system-terminated-130k-channels-in-six-months)
- 15 June 2026: The Next Web reported the purge is "punishing the human creators who never showed their faces"; niche educational content has held up better than broad-topic channels. — [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/youtube-ai-slop-crackdown-faceless-creators-collateral-damage); [WebProNews](https://www.webpronews.com/youtubes-war-on-ai-slop-collides-with-faceless-creators-who-built-the-platform-first/)
- **Hand-animated false positive**: Nathan Little's "Sydney the Song Cat", hand-animated for four years, was demonetized on 12 April (2026) for "inauthentic content"; appeals got templated responses with no specific violation cited. — [Cartoon Brew](https://www.cartoonbrew.com/shorts/nathan-little-sydney-the-song-cat-youtube-260600.html)
- Other popular animated channels were demonetized; one original 3D animation channel was treated as a content farm; another animation creator was demonetized under "harmful content involving minors". — [Creator Handbook](https://www.creatorhandbook.net/youtube-demonitizes-popular-animated-channels/)
- A creator reported their violation label changing from "inauthentic content" to "unsatisfying or off-putting content" after the July 2026 rewording. — [subsub.io](https://www.subsub.io/blog/youtube-channel-reviews-unsatisfying-off-putting-content); [Dexerto](https://www.dexerto.com/youtube/youtube-cracks-down-on-unsatisfying-or-off-putting-videos-with-demonetization-3387786/)

Starcevic Filipovic](https://mateostarcevicfilipovic.medium.com/youtube-quietly-demonetizes-faceless-ai-channels-since-july-2025-30b3d2daaf2e)

### Inferences
- Two different risks: (a) **demonetization** (YPP removal/rejection under inauthentic/generic-repetitive or reused content) — the realistic risk for a solo stick-figure channel; (b) **termination** (spam/deceptive practices) — reported mainly for large-scale networks, fake trailers, and multi-channel clusters. Running several near-identical stickman channels from the same accounts/infrastructure moves a creator toward (b).
- Reported survivor traits (from commentary, not official): narrow educational niche, visible original scripting/research, varied structure between videos, human editorial input. This is creator/analyst opinion.
- The Sydney the Song Cat case shows that even 100% hand-made work with a consistent style can be flagged and that appeals may be opaque; a creator should keep process evidence (project files, script drafts, recordings) for appeal videos.

## 4. Disclosure rules for altered/synthetic content; application to stick figures and AI voices

### Takeaway
Disclosure is required only for realistic altered/synthetic content a viewer could mistake for a real person, place or event. Clearly unrealistic or animated content (stick figures) and AI used for production assistance are exempt; a generic TTS voice is reportedly exempt, but cloning a real person's voice is not.

### Cited Findings
- YouTube blog (policy introduced March 2024): creators need not disclose content that is "clearly unrealistic, animated, includes special effects, or has used generative AI for production assistance"; example: "animation or someone riding a unicorn through a fantastical world". — [YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/disclosing-ai-generated-content)
- Disclosure is required for realistic content, including "synthetically generating a person's voice for narration" where it uses/imitates a real person's voice. — [YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/disclosing-ai-generated-content); [TechSpot](https://www.techspot.com/news/102315-youtube-introduces-labeling-requirements-synthetic-altered-content.html)
- Third-party reading: voice rules turn on **whose** voice it is, not how human it sounds; cloning your own voice is exempt; text-to-speech in a clearly synthetic voice not impersonating anyone does not need disclosure; a synthetic voice built to sound like a recognizable real person needs a label. — [air.io 2026 disclosure guide](https://air.io/en/youtube-hacks/youtube-ai-disclosure-in-2026-label-it-yourself-or-youtube-will); [subscribr](https://subscribr.ai/youtube-strategy/youtube-ai-disclosure-rules)
- Beauty filters, color/lighting adjustments and special effects are exempt. — [YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/disclosing-ai-generated-content)
- A **May 2026** update reportedly renamed the label (to "AI content") and changed examples; the air.io title implies YouTube may apply the label itself if the creator does not. One source lists "AI-generated or altered animation of a missile in a fully animated video" as requiring disclosure, which conflicts with the blanket animation exemption. **[UNVERIFIED]** — [minimatters](https://minimatters.com/?p=6160); [air.io](https://air.io/en/youtube-hacks/youtube-ai-disclosure-in-2026-label-it-yourself-or-youtube-will)

### Inferences
- A stylised stick-figure video with a stock TTS voice most likely needs no disclosure. Disclosure would become necessary if the video uses a cloned voice of a real person, or realistic synthetic footage/images of real events or people (e.g. photorealistic AI inserts in a history or news explainer).
- Disclosure (the label) and monetization eligibility are separate regimes: being exempt from disclosure gives no protection under the inauthentic/generic-repetitive policy.

### Gaps
- Official Help Center wording (answer/14328491) and the exact content of the May 2026 update could not be retrieved.
- Penalties for non-disclosure were not confirmed this session. Background knowledge, unverified: YouTube stated it may add labels itself and that repeated failure to disclose can lead to content removal or YPP suspension.

## 5. "Made for kids" (COPPA) risk and revenue impact

### Takeaway
Animated characters alone do not make content "made for kids"; classification depends on a set of FTC factors (subject matter, child-oriented activities, music, intended audience). If a channel is set or reclassified as made for kids, personalized ads and most engagement/fan-funding features are turned off, with reported ad revenue losses of roughly 50–90%.

### Cited Findings
- FTC factors: subject matter, visual content, use of animated characters or child-oriented activities and incentives, music, age of models, etc. Animated characters or bright colors alone do not automatically bring content under COPPA; the FTC acknowledges animation that appeals to everyone. — [FTC-hosted paper, "COPPAcalypse" (G. Johnson)](https://ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/COPPAcalypse-the-YouTube-settlements-impact-on-kids-content-garrett-a.-johnson.pdf); [Animator Island](https://www.animatorisland.com/?p=14070)
- Animated content does not automatically mean made for kids (South Park, Rick and Morty cited as examples). — [air.io glossary](https://air.io/en/youtube-glossary/what-does-made-for-kids-mean-on-youtube)
- On made-for-kids videos, personalized ads are switched off and only contextual ads run. — [makeinfluence](https://www.makeinfluence.com/en/academy/youtubes-made-for-kids-coppa-classification-what-it-does-to-ad-revenue)
- Revenue impact estimates: **60–90%** ad revenue decline under contextual-only ads (FTC-hosted presentation); **50–80%** less per view (third-party guide); anecdotal creator claims of >95%. — [FTC-hosted paper](https://ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/COPPAcalypse-the-YouTube-settlements-impact-on-kids-content-garrett-a.-johnson.pdf); [subscribr](https://subscribr.ai/youtube-strategy/youtube-made-for-kids-monetization-rules); [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/make-money-kids-youtube-channel/)
- Comments, notifications, end screens, and fan funding (memberships, Super Chat) are disabled on made-for-kids content; direct brand sponsorships are paid outside YouTube's ad system and are structurally unaffected. — [makeinfluence](https://www.makeinfluence.com/en/academy/youtubes-made-for-kids-coppa-classification-what-it-does-to-ad-revenue); [wildandfreetools](https://wildandfreetools.com/blog/youtube-monetization-for-kids-channels/)

### Inferences
- Stick-figure explainers on psychology/finance/history for adults are unlikely to be made for kids. The risk rises for stick fights with cartoon violence, game-character tie-ins (Minecraft/Roblox-style), nursery-like music, simple slapstick with no dialogue, and titles/thumbnails aimed at children — these match several FTC factors.
- Because audience setting is a creator declaration that YouTube can override, a stick-fight channel should decide its target audience deliberately; mis-declaring carries legal (FTC) exposure for the creator, not just revenue impact.

### Gaps
- Official YouTube Help wording on the made-for-kids determination (answer/9528076) was not retrieved.
- No 2025–2026 data on the revenue gap specific to stick-fight channels. The cited decline estimates derive largely from the 2020 change.
- Background knowledge, unverified this session: YouTube also runs "quality principles for kids and family content", under which low-quality kids content can lose monetization; and amended FTC COPPA rules took effect in 2025 — current compliance details should be checked.

## 6. Copyright/trademark risks: copying competitors' characters or scripts; using known stick characters

### Takeaway
Almost nothing specific was found in sources this session; the only cited item is that Alan Becker used copyright reports against duplicate uploads of his animations. Treat this section as largely unanswered.

### Cited Findings
- Alan Becker reported duplicate copies of his animations as copyright infringement, which also helped his official channel rise in search results (fan-wiki statement, weakly sourced). — [Animator vs. Animation Wiki](https://animatorvsanimation.fandom.com/wiki/Alan_Becker)
- "Animator vs. Animation" was first uploaded 3 June 2006 (to Newgrounds) and was widely reuploaded by third parties. — [Know Your Meme](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/animator-vs-animation)
- YouTube's reused content policy (separate from copyright) covers use of others' material without significant original commentary or modification, and can remove monetization even without a copyright claim. — [keepmonetized](https://keepmonetized.com/guides/reused-vs-inauthentic-content/)

### Inferences
- A generic stick figure is too simple to be protectable on its own, but distinctive characters (named, colored, with consistent traits — e.g. Becker's "The Second Coming", Henry Stickmin, Stick War's Order units) and their storylines, artwork, and names are protectable expression/trademarks. This is general legal reasoning, not a sourced finding; it needs a legal source before publication.
- Copying a competitor's script (or having AI paraphrase it) carries three stacked risks: copyright takedown/strike from the competitor, "reused content" findings at YPP review, and detection as near-duplicate/mass-produced content.
- Three copyright strikes lead to channel termination under YouTube's general rules (background knowledge; not sourced this session).

### Gaps
- No source found on fan-content policies of Stick War (Max Games Studios), Henry Stickmin (PuffballsUnited/InnerSloth), or Alan Becker's stance on fan animations vs. monetized use.
- No documented case found of a stickman channel receiving strikes or losing monetization for copying a competitor's character design or scripts.
- No source on how copyright applies to AI-generated stick-figure assets (authorship/ownership) was collected.

## 8. Vietnam-based creators joining the YouTube Partner Program

### Takeaway
Standard thresholds apply (1,000 subscribers + 4,000 watch hours in 12 months or 10M Shorts views in 90 days for ad revenue); Google withholds US tax on earnings from US viewers at 0–30% depending on treaty status, and up to 24% of worldwide earnings if no tax form is filed. No source found this session states Vietnam's rate; for a US-targeted channel this is a major cost item to verify.

### Cited Findings
- Ad-revenue tier: **1,000 subscribers** plus **4,000 valid public watch hours in 12 months** or **10 million valid public Shorts views in 90 days**; confirmed as current requirements in Vietnamese press. — [VietnamNet](https://vietnamnet.vn/en/cheaper-ad-free-youtube-plan-launches-in-vietnam-2544316.html); [Google Help, expanded YPP overview](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13429240?hl=en)
- Expanded (lower) tier for fan funding and Shopping: **500 subscribers**, 3 valid public uploads in the last 90 days, and 3,000 valid public watch hours in 12 months or 3 million Shorts views in 90 days — only "where the expanded program is available"; Vietnam's availability was not confirmed. — [Google Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13429240?hl=en)
- One source claims thresholds will change on **1 February 2027** to 1,000 subscribers + 8,000 watch hours or 20M Shorts views. **[UNVERIFIED — single source; verify before relying on it]** — [search summary; see air.io timeline](https://air.io/en/monetization/youtube-monetization-policy-changes-2026-a-complete-dated-timeline)
- AdSense minimum payment threshold cited as **$100** (US-focused guide). — [1of10](https://1of10.com/blog/youtube-partner-program/)
- Google withholds US tax on earnings non-US creators (explicitly including those in Vietnam) generate from US viewers. — [Hanoi Times](https://hanoitimes.vn/google-withholds-taxes-from-non-us-youtube-creators-317614.html)
- Withholding ranges **0–30%** on US-viewer earnings depending on tax treaty; W-8BEN is the form used to claim a treaty rate; without tax info the default is up to **24% of worldwide earnings**. — [Google AdSense Help](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/14131950); [air.io W-8BEN guide](https://air.io/en/monetization/youtube-tax-info-w-8ben)
- The rate follows tax residence, not passport; Google says it cannot determine treaty eligibility for the creator. — [air.io](https://air.io/en/monetization/youtube-tax-info-w-8ben); [Google AdSense Help](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/14131950)
- Tax forms reportedly need periodic resubmission (a Google help page mentions resubmitting before 10 December). — [Google Help (zh-Hant)](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10390801?hl=zh-Hant)

### Inferences
- Because the target audience is US/UK, the US-viewer share of revenue will be large, so the withholding rate applies to most of the channel's best-paying views. If Vietnam has no treaty in force (see Gaps), effective loss approaches 30% of US-sourced ad revenue; UK-viewer revenue is not subject to US withholding.
- Filing the W-8BEN promptly matters even without treaty benefits, since not filing exposes worldwide earnings to 24% withholding instead of only the US portion.

### Gaps
- **Vietnam's actual withholding rate was not found in any source this session.** Background knowledge, unverified: the US–Vietnam income tax treaty was signed in 2015 but has not entered into force, which would mean the 30% statutory rate applies to US-viewer earnings of Vietnam-resident creators. Must be confirmed with Google's tax tool/IRS or a tax adviser.
- Not found: whether the 500-subscriber expanded tier is available in Vietnam; Vietnam-specific AdSense payout methods (wire transfer), PIN/address verification delays, and Vietnamese personal income tax/VAT treatment of AdSense income (Vietnam's tax authority has pursued tax collection from YouTube earners — background knowledge, unsourced here).
- Not found: whether a Vietnam location, VPN use or US-targeted metadata affects YPP review. Any such claims are creator speculation.
