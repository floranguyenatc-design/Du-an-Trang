## 1. What end-to-end workflows do creators use in 2025–2026?

### Takeaway
The dominant 2026 pipeline is: LLM (ChatGPT/Claude) writes the script and a per-scene prompt list → an image model (most often Nano Banana / Nano Banana Pro, via Gemini, Google Flow or OpenArt) draws one stick-figure image per scene from a locked character reference → optionally an image-to-video model (Kling, Veo in Flow) adds a few seconds of motion per image → TTS voice (ElevenLabs, or free Fish Audio / Edge TTS) → assembly in CapCut with the voiceover laid first and visuals synced to it. Several tutorials advise generating the voiceover before visuals and mapping scenes to timestamps.

### Cited Findings
- Generic 2026 faceless pipeline reported by multiple guides: pick niche → script (AI drafts outline) → voiceover with a tool like ElevenLabs → visuals → caption/edit in CapCut → repurpose into Shorts. — [Dupple, "How to Make AI YouTube Videos: Full Workflow (2026)"](https://dupple.com/learn/how-to-make-ai-youtube-videos); [dev.to guide](https://dev.to/max_quimby/how-to-start-a-faceless-youtube-channel-with-ai-step-by-step-14m5)

## 2. Which image/video models handle stick-figure/doodle style well, and what prompt patterns keep the character consistent?

### Takeaway
Tutorials converge on Nano Banana / Nano Banana Pro for the stills and Kling or Veo (in Google Flow) for motion, with consistency enforced by (1) one canonical reference image/character sheet reused in every generation and (2) rigid per-scene prompt templates that restate the locked design, "ONE stickman only", pose, background, props and negative rules. Verbatim example prompts could not be retrieved; only the template rules are documented below.

### Cited Findings
- Models named in stickman-specific tutorials: Nano Banana Pro (via OpenArt) for images and Kling AI for animation. — [randomprompts.org](https://randomprompts.org/blog/how-to-make-animation-stickman-ai-guide); Meta AI and Google Flow for free-tier visuals — [givenprompt.com](https://givenprompt.com/how-to-start-a-faceless-youtube-channel-with-stick-figure-animation-100-free); Google Flow, Kling, Runway or Luma as targets for ~10-second clip prompts — [varg.ai](https://varg.ai/how-to/skills/stickman-video-director)
- A widely circulated "stickman animation video generator" master prompt (GitHub repo and a near-identical gist "pulled from a YT video tutorial") sets the LLM up as a "video workflow generator" following a strict step-by-step workflow. Its rules, as quoted in search snippets: "First, upload a reference image of your stickman character. The stickman must look identical in every scene" (same proportions, line style, colour, features); "Every image prompt includes explicit subject count ("ONE stickman only") to prevent duplicates, plus full pose, background, props, composition, and negative rules. Every animation prompt states its EXACT duration in seconds"; animation prompts also state camera direction and ambient sound with no music; "The voiceover script is a standalone section after all scene prompts, with a timing map"; it also outputs thumbnail/hook-frame prompts. — [GitHub azeemgujjar/STICKMAN-ANIMATION-VIDEO-GENERATOR-PROMPT](https://github.com/azeemgujjar/STICKMAN-ANIMATION-VIDEO-GENERATOR-PROMPT); [gist whitelotusapps](https://gist.github.com/whitelotusapps/d958cb4766d3fb1aa41030bec37cffb4)
- General Nano Banana consistency method (not stickman-specific, tutorial dated Jan 2026): make a character reference sheet from a single image at 16:9, then use that sheet as an "ingredient" for start frames and video; with several characters, upload each character's sheet as a separate ingredient. — [lilys.ai recap of AI Video School](https://lilys.ai/notes/en/nano-banana-20260127/consistent-character-sheets-nano-banana); [videohighlight.com](https://videohighlight.com/v/f4HcdR3cd4M)

- Nano Banana is paired with Veo 3.1 or Kling for video while reusing the same character sheet as reference. — [videohighlight.com (Veo 3.1 character consistency)](https://videohighlight.com/v/o9QDE-q16i8)

- A vendor article is dedicated to "AI Stick Figure Animation: Same Character, Every Shot" and suggests a new shot every couple of sentences. — [bulkimagen.com](https://bulkimagen.com/blog/ai-stick-figure-animation)
- Additional prompt libraries exist (not opened): a stickman style library/prompt generator repo, media.io's "prompt formula" page for simple stick-figure clips, and a paid "Viral Stickman Animation Prompt Pack". — [GitHub Ijazkhan40](https://github.com/Ijazkhan40/stickman-video-prompt-generator); [media.io](https://www.media.io/ai-prompts/stick-figure-animation-prompts.html); [getsauce.in](https://www.getsauce.in/digitools/viral-stickman-animation-prompt-pack-for-faceless-ai-channels)

### Inferences
- A workable per-scene prompt skeleton implied by the circulating master prompt: [reference image attached] + "ONE stickman only, identical to reference (proportions, line weight, colour, face)" + pose/action + background + props + composition/aspect ratio + negative rules (no extra figures, no text, no style change). This is my reconstruction from the quoted rules, not a verbatim prompt.
- Reference-image conditioning (Nano Banana "ingredients"/image input) rather than text-only description is what makes hundreds of matching frames feasible; text-only prompting is nowhere recommended in the sources found.

## 3. Are there dedicated stick-figure tools, and how do they compare in price and effort?

### Takeaway
Three families exist: traditional animation apps (Pivot Animator, Stick Nodes, FlipaClip, Adobe Animate — cheap or free but high manual effort), whiteboard tools (VideoScribe, roughly $10–42/month), and a 2026 crop of AI "stickman video generator" SaaS at about $15–62/month for entry/mid tiers that turn a script into a narrated stick-figure video. Traditional frame-by-frame work is far slower than the AI image route.

## 4. How many images/scenes does an 8–15 minute video need, and what are realistic cost and hours per video? Outsourcing rates?

### Takeaway
Sources put a 10-minute stick-figure video at roughly 100–200 images when one image is drawn per narration line (as few as 40–60 shots at slower pacing; up to ~240 at the fastest), i.e. about one new image every 3–6 seconds. Per-video tool cost is low — near zero on free tiers with daily caps, or a share of a $20–$100/month stack; at API rates, 100–200 Nano Banana Pro images cost about $13–27. Hours per video are poorly documented (vendor claims "under two hours"; one anecdote of 15 minutes of editing per finished minute). Outsourced editing is quoted at $10–$150 per video.

### Cited Findings

## 5. Static images with simple motion versus full animation: what do successful channels actually do?

### Takeaway
Evidence is indirect: the tools and tutorials built around this niche overwhelmingly produce a sequence of still stick-figure images timed to narration (one per line), with motion added either by editor keyframes or by short image-to-video clips; true character animation is treated as optional. I found no source that audits what specific top channels do.

### Cited Findings
- Dedicated stickman SaaS products work by splitting the script into scenes and drawing one new still image per scene/line, timed to narration. — [stickreel.com](https://stickreel.com/how-to-make-a-stickman-animation); [yourfacelessfactory.com](https://www.yourfacelessfactory.com/); [embertube.com](https://embertube.com/stick-figure-video-generator)
- Tutorials that add motion do it per image with an image-to-video model at default settings (Kling) or as ~10-second clips (Flow, Kling, Runway, Luma). — [randomprompts.org](https://randomprompts.org/blog/how-to-make-animation-stickman-ai-guide); [varg.ai](https://varg.ai/how-to/skills/stickman-video-director)
- CapCut keyframes (position, scale, rotation, opacity, with ease curves) are the documented way to add zoom/pan/pop-in motion to stills. — [capcut.com keyframe tool](https://www.capcut.com/tools/keyframe-animation)
- Vendors argue script and angle matter more than the drawing: a channel with a researched script per video, a clear angle and non-repeating topics is better positioned than one reusing a template. — [stickreel.com](https://stickreel.com/how-to-make-a-stickman-animation)
- Saturaai's framing: the Canva stickman format can work for faceless channels "if the packaging and retention systems are tight"; the format works because it compresses production cost. — [saturaai.com (Canva)](https://saturaai.com/blog/how-to-launch-stickman-animation-videos-without-studio-free-canva-workflow-for-you-esta4y); [saturaai.com (why breaking out)](https://saturaai.com/blog/why-stick-figure-youtube-videos-keep-breaking-out-in-2026-jpkuyd)
- A case write-up exists on a viral AI stick-figure series ("Stickocalypse"). — [vuela.ai](https://vuela.ai/blog/how-to-make-viral-stick-figure-series-with-ai)
- Outlier caution: a tutorial creator claimed a stickman channel "made over $900" and gained 95,000 subscribers in its first month; the reviewer says the production claim holds but the channel is an outlier among thousands of near-identical uploads. Other unverified claims: a stickman channel with 250,000+ subscribers at ~$12,000/month (Medium post). — [Income Reality Check](https://income-reality-check.pages.dev/a/how-to-create-stickman-animation-with-ai-100-free/); [Medium](https://medium.com/no-time/how-to-build-a-profitable-stickman-animation-channel-using-ai-5fb2a48595d7)

### Inferences
- The economics in section 4 (100–200 images per 10 minutes) only make sense with stills plus light motion; full image-to-video for every scene multiplies cost and generation time and introduces style drift, so it is most likely reserved for hooks or key beats.
- Given YouTube's "inauthentic content" policy, differentiation is likelier to come from script quality and pacing than from animation fidelity.

### Gaps
- No source directly analysed named successful stick-figure channels' visuals (static vs animated share, cuts per minute). This needs manual viewing of top channels.
- The Saturaai "why stick-figure videos keep breaking out" article and the Stickocalypse case could not be opened for channel names and stats.

### Inferences
- DIY monthly stack arithmetic: $20 + $19.99 + $22 = $61.99/month; at 8 videos/month that is about $7.75 of subscriptions per video, before any video-model credits.
- Google AI Pro's 1,000 Flow credits equal at most 100 Veo 3.1 Lite clips (10 credits each) or 10 Quality clips (100 each) per month — enough to animate a handful of key scenes per video, not every scene of several long videos.
